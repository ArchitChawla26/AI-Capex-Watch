Methodology & verification

Source. All data comes from the SEC EDGAR company facts API (data.sec.gov), which republishes the XBRL facts in each company's 10-Q and 10-K filings. Nothing here is estimated or taken from a data vendor. Figures are as filed; if a company restates, the next weekly refresh picks the restated value up.

Tags (us-gaap). Capex: PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment — for Amazon, PaymentsToAcquireProductiveAssets, the tag Amazon reports under today. Revenue: RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax, falling back to Revenues / SalesRevenueNet — Alphabet reported under the first tag through Q1 2025 and the second after, so the series are merged by period. Operating cash flow: NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities.

From year-to-date filings to single quarters. Cash-flow items in a 10-Q are cumulative year-to-date amounts, not single quarters. Single quarters are recovered by differencing consecutive year-to-date values within the fiscal year: Q1 = Q1 YTD; Q2 = 6M − Q1; Q3 = 9M − 6M; Q4 = the 10-K full-year figure − 9M. Only spans beginning on the fiscal-year start date are treated as cumulatives — Amazon also tags trailing-twelve-month capex spans in its 10-Q supplements, and those are excluded from the derivation and used as a cross-check instead (below).

Verification. For every company, the sum of my four derived quarters for fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2025 is reconciled to the fiscal-year capex fact in the company's 10-K. Amazon's filer-tagged TTM capex spans give a second, independent check on the built series.

Limitations — read these. (1) Capex here is total purchases of property and equipment, not AI-only spend. The filings do not split capex into AI and non-AI; data centers dominate the recent growth, but warehouses (Amazon), offices and other assets are in these numbers too. This is the single biggest limitation of any tracker built on filed data, including this one. (2) It is cash capex: assets acquired under finance leases are excluded, so headline "capex" figures some companies quote (which include finance leases) run higher — e.g. Meta's 2024 figure is $37.3B here vs the $39.2B including finance leases cited in its release. (3) Revenue is total company revenue, not AI or cloud segment revenue, so the conversion ratios are directional evidence about the whole business, not a return on AI investment in isolation — Amazon's ratio in particular is flattered by its retail base. (4) Capex leads revenue by construction and installation lags, so a point-in-time spread can look worse than the steady state; that is why I track the trend, not one quarter.
